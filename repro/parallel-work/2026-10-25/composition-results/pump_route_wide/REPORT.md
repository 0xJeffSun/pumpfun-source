The new substantive conclusions are:

- Multiply is modulo 2^128, with no error tag; zero high inputs make its 64-by-64 product exact. Full high-input contributions and discarded bit-128 products are accounted for.
- Division returns full 128-bit floor quotient for every unsigned N and D>0. Its core also returns remainder. The ledger covers small-word division, both quotient-estimation/correction arms, iterative reduction, carry/borrow and termination; no transitive arithmetic call remains uninterpreted.
- D=0 is not a uniform application error: (N,D)=(0,0) returns quotient 1/remainder 0. Positive N reaches mod64-by-zero at 0x1b4658 or 0x1b4788. The exact VM fault/result rule is not inferred from opcode bytes. All admitted application division calls exclude zero first.
- Checked ceil adds N+D before subtracting one; overflow precedes sum-zero, which precedes denominator-zero. Therefore ceil128(0,0) selects the subtraction failure, and H/1 fails at the intermediate sum even though the mathematical quotient fits.
- For u64 fee operands, product plus 10000 cannot overflow u128; only the final u64 fee narrowing can fail arithmetically. Budget's denominator carry is preserved, and its ignored high quotient word is provably zero because E<=B. Fees are computed before correction and are not recomputed. Both constant-product adapters preserve 65-bit denominators and produce quotients bounded by their respective u64 reserves.
